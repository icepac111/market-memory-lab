I wanted to understand what the U.S. 10-year nominal Treasury yield and 10-year real TIPS yield reveal when they move together or apart.

I analyzed 5,932 common valid observations from 2003-01-02 through 2026-09-17 using Federal Reserve H.15 constant-maturity yield series. Missing observations were not filled.

On the latest common date, the nominal yield was 4.94% and the real yield was 2.61%. Their difference was 2.33 percentage points, or 233 basis points.

That difference is inflation compensation, not exact expected inflation. It can also reflect inflation risk premium, relative liquidity, market technicals, and measurement effects.

The harder test was whether changes between successive common observations moved together. The full-sample Pearson correlation was 0.94 in levels and 0.81 in successive changes. The rolling cross-correlation also moved through a range rather than remaining constant, which is why a single full-sample number should not be treated as a permanent market law.

The molecular visual is an intuition layer for the measured structure. The dotted arrows show candidate influences, not identified causal effects.

What this evidence does not establish:
• future inflation
• future Treasury or TIPS returns
• causation
• investment superiority
• a trading signal
• a buy or sell recommendation

This is an educational market-structure study, not individualized investment advice.
